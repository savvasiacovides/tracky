Tyres

Nothing you bolt on changes a lap time as much as the tyres. Choose by how you use the car: for each, the one we would buy, set against the alternatives. Tracky shows the difference: put the tyres in your car’s details, then compare a session on the old set with one on the new.

A road car with a few track days

You drive to work on them, in the rain, all year round, and take the car to a circuit a few times a season. You need a road tyre first that copes with a hot lap.

Our pick: Continental SportContact 7. It came first in ADAC’s 2025 test of 18 tyres and in EVO’s 2024 test, and across 22 wet-braking tests it stopped shorter than its premium rivals on average.

The Michelin Pilot Sport 4S is the long-standing favourite, made by Michelin for road and occasional track use, with a 300 treadwear grade. The Goodyear Eagle F1 SuperSport leans further towards dry grip, so it suits a car that rarely sees rain.

Why not a track tyre? A semi-slick wears fast on the road and has far less grip in the rain. If the car is your daily driver, a road tyre from this group is the safer choice.

Regular track days, driving there and back

You do a track day most months and drive to the circuit. These are road-legal semi-slicks: much more grip on a dry track, less tread and less grip in the rain.

Our pick: Pirelli P Zero Trofeo RS. It won Auto Bild’s 2026 track-day tyre test and Tyre Reviews’ 2024 track-day test.

The Michelin Pilot Sport Cup 2 is the benchmark many drivers know, and Michelin makes it for road and regular track use. The Bridgestone Potenza Race was Bridgestone’s first tyre made for track days. The Goodyear Eagle F1 SuperSport R stopped shortest in the wet in Tyre Reviews’ 2024 test, so it is the one to choose if your track days are often damp.

On a semi-slick, drive the first laps gently while the tyres warm up, set the pressures when they are hot, and take it easy on wet roads on the way home. Check the tread after every track day.

Smaller cars, budgets and 200-treadwear classes

An MX-5, a GR86, a Civic, a Clio: lighter cars, smaller wheels, and often a racing class that only allows tyres with a treadwear of 200 or more.

Our pick: Yokohama Advan A052. Yokohama makes it for the classes that require a 200-treadwear tyre and says it grips in the wet as well as the dry.

The Yokohama Advan Neova AD09 has the same 200 treadwear and is Yokohama’s street-legal sports tyre for road and track, so it suits a fast road car that also does track days. The Toyo Proxes R888R grips harder at 100 treadwear and is road legal, but it is weak in the rain, so keep it for dry track days.

A track car on dry days

The car mostly lives at the circuit and you pick your days. These are the most extreme road-legal tyres: the fastest on a dry track, and the least forgiving in the wet or when cold.

Our pick: Michelin Pilot Sport Cup 2 R. Michelin makes it for intense track use and it beat the Goodyear Eagle F1 SuperSport RS and the Pirelli P Zero Trofeo R in Tyre Reviews’ 2020 head-to-head.

The SuperSport RS had the sharpest steering in that test. The Dunlop Sport Maxx Race 2 was developed with Porsche for the 911 GT3 and is approved for the 718 Cayman GT4 and GT4 RS in 245/35 R20 and 295/30 R20.

How we choose

By use, not by brand

The right tyre depends on how often you are on track, whether you drive there, and how often it rains.

Tests, not hype

Our picks follow independent tests and what each maker says the tyre is for. We name the test beside every result.
