Gear that finds time

Tracky times laps with the iPhone alone. This is the gear that makes the laps more exact, adds engine data and video and shows how hard you are working. Tyres have their own page. For each, the one we would buy, set against the alternatives.

“Tested with Tracky” means we have used it with Tracky in our own cars. Everything else here works with Tracky’s published support but has not been through our own testing yet, and we say so.

GPS receiver: sharper lap times

The iPhone gives Tracky one position a second. At 160 km/h that is 44 metres between points, so the line, the sectors and the lap time are partly guessed. A 25 Hz receiver gives Tracky a point every 1.8 metres at the same speed.

Our pick: RaceBox Mini. It is the receiver Tracky is developed and tested with, Tracky wakes it when you press START, and it fixes the biggest weakness of timing with a phone.

Buy the Mini S instead only if you want the receiver to keep recording when the phone is not in the car. The Dual XGPS160 gives 10 positions a second, works with many other apps and is approved by Apple for use with Tracky, but we have not tested it with Tracky yet.

OBD reader: see what the engine did

Your lap tells you where you were slow. The OBD reader tells you why: revs, throttle, speed and temperatures from the car itself, on the same graph as every lap.

Our pick: OBDLink MX+. It is the reader we use in our own cars.

In our own car it delivered about 30 rev readings a second. It also reads more of a car’s networks than the others here. If you want to spend less, the Carista OBD works with Tracky and is a fraction of the price. The OBDLink CX suits BMW and MINI owners who also code their car with BimmerCode.

Camera: watch every lap back

Video shows what the data cannot: your hands, the kerbs, the traffic. Tracky puts your speed, gap and revs over the video and lines it up with the lap.

Our pick: GoPro MISSION 1 PRO. It is the camera we use.

Tracky starts and stops it when you press START, so you never forget to record, and its 1-inch sensor keeps detail in shade and at dusk. The HERO13 Black costs less, is started by Tracky too, and has built-in GPS. The DJI Osmo Action 6 has an excellent picture, but Tracky cannot start it: you add its video from Photos afterwards.

Heart-rate strap: see the stress

Your heart rate shows where a lap is hard work: the corners you brace for, the traffic you fight, the stint where you start to tire. Tracky records it with every lap. It stays private unless you choose to share it.

Our pick: Garmin HRM 600. It is the strap we drive with.

It charges like a phone instead of taking coin cells, lasts up to two months at an hour a day, and the strap goes in the wash once the pod is off. It also talks to Tracky and a Garmin watch at the same time. The Polar H10 is the strap others measure their accuracy against and costs less. Choose the Polar Verity Sense if a strap round your chest is uncomfortable under a harness: it is worn on the arm, but an arm sensor is optical and less exact than a chest strap. Garmin’s cheaper HRM 200 gives the same heart rate with a coin cell instead of a charger.
